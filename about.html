Mi’Lah “mee-lah” Clark, also known as Artoflah, is a multidisciplinary graphic designer working between New Jersey and New York. She is currently studying Communication Design at Parsons School of Design with a minor in Fine Arts, creating work across book design, typography, print, web, UX/UI, motion, object-making, and whatever strange format the idea starts asking for.

Her work is driven by color, tiny details, craft, and experiments that sometimes begin with no faith they’ll work. She likes building playful, thoughtful design systems that feel expressive, handmade, and a little unexpected.

Mi’Lah is a multidisciplinary designer who turns research, stories, and half-formed ideas into expressive visual systems. Her work moves between brand identity, editorial design, typography, photography, motion, digital experiences, and physical objects, with a strong focus on craft, concept, and the small details that make a project feel alive.

She is especially interested in work that can be read, held, clicked, watched, or experienced. Whether she is designing a publication, prototyping an interface, directing an image, or coding an interaction, she approaches each project with curiosity, humor, and a willingness to experiment until the idea finds its right form.

She is currently looking for internship opportunities and is open to freelance work and creative collaborations.
